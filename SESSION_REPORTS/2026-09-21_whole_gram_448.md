## The law, as built

1. THE WHOLE GRAM, SHOWN. `formatGrams` is a whole gram, round half up, thousands grouped. `formatGramsWhole` is the same function. Pounds stay two decimals (`formatLbs` / `format2`). Percents, hours, days, money, and instrument readings are not sent through `formatGrams`.
2. THE WHOLE GRAM, STORED. `roundGrams` is an integer. `sumGrams` adds those integers. `parseGrams` (every form door) uses it. Values already stored are not rewritten. They display rounded.
3. THE 448 POUND. `GRAMS_PER_LB = 448` in `src/config.ts`. `LB_GRAMS`, compliance `GRAMS_PER_LB`, and `ZOHO_GRAMS_PER_LB` re-export it. A stored 1,234.56 g shows as 1,235 g and 2.76 lb. 50 g is 0.11 lb. 15,000 lb of silo capacity is 6,720,000 g. 4,800 lb of FFZ capacity is 2,150,400 g.
4. METRC-FACING WEIGHTS. `metrcQuantity` keeps two decimals for METRC CSVs, the New Packages sheet, and the METRC mirror sums. Nothing in those paths converts at 448. Where METRC demands pounds, the factor is METRC's own 453.59237, named in the comment on `metrcQuantity`. No live path converts to METRC pounds, so that number appears only in that comment.
5. MACHINE FEEDS. `/feeds/trim-weighs.json` sends the stored gram number. `/silos/live.json` sends the stored gram numbers. The labels on those payloads (`wetG`, the pound strings) are the person-facing figures.

People-facing report CSVs and the inventory sheet CSV use `formatGrams` (a comma groups the cell, so the CSV quotes it). The gram number format in a people workbook is `#,##0`.

## Assumptions for the owner's word

- Pounds are computed from the gram number passed in, at 448, then shown to two decimals. A stored 1,234.56 g is 2.76 lb, which is the stored grams divided by 448, not a second rounding of the displayed 1,235.
- A METRC file of a weight that entered after this deploy carries that whole gram at two decimals (`1000.00`). A METRC file built straight from a stored decimal (no door in between) keeps those decimals. The mirror keeps METRC's quantities.
- The trim-weigh feed and the numeric fields on `/silos/live.json` are the stored numbers. The words on the tiles are whole grams and two-decimal pounds.
- People CSVs quote a gram cell when the thousands separator puts a comma in it.
- `parseGrams` is the form door, so a typed 250.75 is stored as 251. An edit that rounds to the gram already stored is refused as unchanged.
- The sheet-sync exports and Spanish lines were restored because `src/sheetsync.ts` already called them and the tree did not typecheck without them. They are not part of the gram ruling.
