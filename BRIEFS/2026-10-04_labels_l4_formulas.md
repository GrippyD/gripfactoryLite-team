## The law

1. A FORMULA is a data object's content (text box, memo, barcode, QR) whose first character is `=`. Content that does not start with `=` is text with tokens, exactly as today: nothing about existing templates changes.
2. THE GRAMMAR, as Excel: function calls `NAME(arg, ...)` with names in any case; text in double quotes (`""` inside is one quote); whole and decimal numbers; field tokens `{key}` and `{d:table:column}` as values (the field's value as L2 resolves it, dates as `YYYY-MM-DD`, blank when unresolved); `&` joins text; calls nest. Square brackets are also accepted around a catalog key or its words, so his `Right([PACKAGE.TAG.ID],6)` style reads: `[package_tag]`, `[Package tag]` and `[PACKAGE.TAG.ID]` (dots and spaces read as one separator, case ignored) are all `{package_tag}`. A name that matches no field is a parse error naming it.
3. THE FUNCTIONS (at least): `LEFT(text, [n=1])`, `RIGHT(text, [n=1])`, `MID(text, start, n)` (start counts from 1), `LEN(text)`, `UPPER`, `LOWER`, `TRIM` (ends and doubled spaces, as Excel), `SUBSTITUTE(text, old, new)`, `CONCAT(a, b, ...)`, `IFBLANK(value, fallback)`, and `TEXT(value, format)`. TEXT's date formats from the parts `YYYY YY MMMM MMM MM M DD D` (case ignored, any separators kept as typed: `"MM/DD/YY"` gives `09/24/26`, `"MMM D, YYYY"` gives `Sep 24, 2026`); its number formats `0`, `0.0`, `0.00`, `0.000` and `#,##0` (rounded half up). A date in TEXT that is not a real date is returned unchanged; a number format on text that is not a number is returned unchanged. Month words are English (labels go to Michigan retail).
4. ONE EVALUATOR. A pure parser and evaluator in its own module (`src/labelformula.ts`): `evaluateFormula(raw, fills) -> { value, error }`, never throwing. `bindText` (and so every print page and the editor's server render) calls it for content that starts with `=`. The editor's client preview runs the SAME code, not a second copy written by hand: generate the client's copy from the module's source (as the client script is already built from a TS function) and prove it with a test that runs one table of cases through both.
5. ERRORS. A formula that does not parse, names an unknown function or field, or has the wrong count of arguments: in the editor the object shows `#ERROR` and the options pane says why in words (EN and ES); Save Template refuses while any object holds a broken formula, naming the object. At print, a blank field never errors (`RIGHT` of blank is blank). A formula's result is capped at 500 characters.
6. CODES. A barcode or QR holding a formula encodes its result; Toggle caption shows the result. Attach shows the attached object's result, not its formula.
7. TOKENS. `layoutTokens` finds the fields inside formulas (both brace and bracket spellings) so Quick Print's preview still warns of blank fields.
8. THE EDITOR. Source Data and Connections insert `{key}` at the cursor when the content is a formula (today they append). Under the content box of a selected text box, memo, barcode or QR, a "Formulas" line opens a short help list: each function with one example from the field catalog (for instance `=RIGHT({package_tag},6)`). "Preview with package" shows results.
9. WORDS: EN and ES for every label, help line, error and refusal.

## The law: ink and fill (his second ask)

10. INK AND FILL. Text Box, Memo, Barcode and QR gain two options in the options pane: Ink (Black, White) and Fill (None, Black, White). The fill paints the object's whole box behind its content; the ink colors the text, the caption, the bars and the QR modules. Black and white only: the labels print on monochrome label printers (a thermal printer prints white by leaving the paper bare), so white ink on a black fill prints as knocked out text. A layout saved before L4 reads as Ink Black, Fill None (no version bump needed: missing keys take those defaults); the layout JSON round-trips both keys.
11. CODES INVERTED. A barcode or QR with White ink keeps its quiet zone inside the box in the fill's color (Code128 ten modules each side, QR four modules), and the options pane says in words that inverted codes scan on fewer scanners, so test the label with the scanner it will meet. White ink with Fill None on a barcode or QR is refused in words (it would print nothing).
12. SHAPES TOO. Shapes' Fill gains White beside None and Black, and their outline takes the same Ink, so a black block with a white border or a white panel on a black label can be drawn.
13. FRONT AND BACK. Objects draw in the order they were placed. The options pane of every selected object gains Bring to front and Send to back, saved in the layout's order, so white text can sit on a black shape drawn first or later.
14. EVERYWHERE THE SAME. The editor, the attach view, the editor's preview with a package and every print page (L1's print, L3's Quick Print) draw ink, fill and order alike; print colors are exact (`print-color-adjust: exact`) so the browser does not drop the black fill.
